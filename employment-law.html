What to Expect

The employment
law process

Employment cases often begin with small warning signs that escalate. Click each step to see what to do and what to avoid.

1

Recognize the Red Flags

Unfair treatment, sudden discipline, harassment, retaliation, unpaid wages, or termination after reporting an issue. Trust your instincts. If something feels wrong, it often is.

2

Document Everything

Keep records of emails, texts, performance reviews, pay stubs, schedules, and any incidents or conversations related to the issue. Documentation can be critical to protecting your claim.

3

Avoid Confrontation Without Legal Guidance

Before confronting your employer or HR, understand your rights. Saying the wrong thing, or too much, can unintentionally weaken your position.

4

Consult an Employment Attorney

An experienced attorney can evaluate your situation, explain your rights under state and federal law, and determine whether your employer's actions are unlawful.

5

Strategic Action or Formal Complaint

Depending on your case, your attorney may recommend internal action, filing with a government agency, or pursuing legal action directly against your employer.

6

Pursue Resolution or Litigation

Many cases resolve through negotiation or settlement; others require litigation. Throughout, your attorney advocates for accountability, compensation, and justice.
